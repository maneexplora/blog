After dinner, already quite full, we stopped at Galia, a small shop on Gran Vía that calls itself «a fábrica de pastéis de nata» (the pastel de nata factory).

## The story of our visit

We only wanted one to share. When I ordered, the seller said: "Just one? You need to make more room, these things are delicious. I'm selling you two." So we took two. He was right.

## What we ordered

- **Two pastéis de nata**: crisp puff pastry and custard caramelised on top.

## The good

- **The flavour.** Excellent.
- **Value for money.** I don't remember the exact price, but it wasn't expensive.
- **The service.** Very friendly, as the story shows.

## Good to know

- The shop is small: you order at the counter and eat standing up or take away.

## The history

According to the shop's tiled mural, it all started in Lisbon, when a young dreamer and the love of his life learned the secret of the perfect pastel de nata from a master craftsman. Almost a century later, the recipe is made in Madrid with respect for Portuguese tradition. *(Source: the shop's own mural.)*

The pastel de nata was born at the Jerónimos Monastery in Belém (Lisbon). When the monastery closed in 1834, the recipe was sold to the owners of a nearby sugar refinery, who opened the Fábrica de Pastéis de Belém in 1837. Only those made there are called «pastéis de Belém»; the rest are «pastéis de nata».

*Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastel_de_nata) and [Portugal Resident](https://www.portugalresident.com/a-pastry-made-by-monks/).*

## Verdict

Excellent. If you're on Gran Vía, order two straight away.
