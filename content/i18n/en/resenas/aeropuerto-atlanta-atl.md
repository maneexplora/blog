After Houston, our second layover on the way to Madrid was Atlanta. This is where we caught Delta's transatlantic flight, and we spent the wait walking through Concourse E.

## A bit of history

The airport was born in 1925, when Mayor Walter A. Sims signed a lease on an abandoned auto racetrack, named Candler Field. The first commercial flight arrived in 1926, carrying mail for Florida Airways. In 1971 it took the name of Mayor William B. Hartsfield, and in 2003 Maynard H. Jackson's name was added. Since 1998 it has been the world's busiest passenger airport. *Source: [Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport](https://www.atl.com/?p=1399).*

## The airport's art

The ATL Airport Art program has more than 1,000 works, one of the largest public art collections in the southeastern United States, and it turns 30 in 2026. *Source: [ATL Airport Art](https://atl.com/about-atl/airport-art-program).*

Here's what we saw:

- **Martin Luther King Jr.: Legacy of a Dream.** An exhibit with photos and objects provided by the King Center, organized around his early life, family life, message, death and honors, and legacy. It has been at the airport since the mid-1980s. We saw one of his suits, his glasses, his watch and segregation-era signs that help you understand the fight for civil rights. *Source: [Waymarking](https://www.waymarking.com/waymarks/WM67K4_Martin_Luther_King_Jr_Concourse_E_ATL_Airport_Atlanta_GA).*
- **Bird Dog and Belle, by Mary Engel.** Engel, originally from Chicago and based in Athens, Georgia, since 1993, has been sculpting animals for more than thirty years. She builds each piece from wire, mesh, plaster and epoxy, and covers it with found objects such as buttons, dog tags and tools. The pieces are on display courtesy of Marcia Wood Gallery. *Source: [Marcia Wood Gallery](https://www.marciawoodgallery.com/artists/41-mary-engel/).*
- **A female figure** made of porcelain figurines, in the same found-object style, and **a textile piece** with white dresses.
- **Beyond the 50: Continuing Hip Hop's Legacy in Atlanta.** An exhibit about EarWax Records, the record store that opened in 1993 on Peachtree Street, hosted artists like OutKast and closed in 2007. It was curated by its former owner, Darryl "Jasz" Smith, with Emory University's Rose Library, and it's on view between gates E11 and E15 through November 2027. *Source: [Emory University](https://news.emory.edu/stories/2025/12/emorys-rose-library-partners-atlanta-airport-earwax-records-hip-hop-exhibit).*
- **The Airport Art display cases**, with colorful contemporary pieces, like a mailbox overflowing with letters.

## The good

- **The art.** There's so much to see: history, contemporary art and hip hop.
- **Wayfinding.** We got to our gate without any trouble.

## Room for improvement

- **The souvenirs.** There are several souvenir shops; to my taste, the souvenir designs have room to grow.
- **Comfort.** In terms of facilities, it's an average airport.

## Verdict

If you have a long layover in Atlanta, don't just sit at the gate: Concourse E is almost a museum, with history, art and music. With so much to see, the wait flies by.
