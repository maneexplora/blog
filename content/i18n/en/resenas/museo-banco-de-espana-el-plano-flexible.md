One evening, passing by Cibeles, we went into the Banco de España exhibition gallery. It was about to close, but they still let us in and helped us. We spent about 30 minutes, entry was free and they even gave us a book about the exhibition. A really nice touch.

## The exhibition

*El plano flexible* (The Pliable Plane) explores textiles as a meeting point between art, architecture and industry. It brings together around fifty works from the 17th to the 21st century: tapestries, carpets, paintings, photographs, videos, documents and coins, from the Banco de España Collection and other collections. It is curated by Yolanda Romero, curator at the Banco de España, and runs until 28 March 2027.

What I liked most was how the works play with fabrics and materials. Next to old Flemish tapestries, like Geraert Peemans' piece showing January and February, there are contemporary works: Aurèlia Muñoz's macramé *Manto* (1973), Teresa Lanceta's *Tejido* (1999) and Antonio Pichillá's *Kukulkan (Feathered Serpent)* (2017). According to its label, Pichillá reinterprets the traditional Tz'utujil Maya warping board and revives the legacy of his grandmother, a weaver.

*Sources: [Banco de España press release](https://www.bde.es/f/webbe/GAP/Secciones/SalaPrensa/NotasInformativas/26/presbe2026-85en.pdf) and the works' labels.*

## Practical information

- **Price:** free, with advance booking ([esMadrid](https://www.esmadrid.com/agenda/plano-flexible-sala-exposiciones-banco-espana)).
- **Hours:** Tuesday to Saturday, 11 am–2 pm and 4–8 pm; closed Mondays ([Banco de España](https://www.bde.es/wbe/es/noticias-eventos/recursos-informativos/videos/inauguracion-de-la-nueva-exposicion-el-plano-flexible.html)).
- **Where:** Plaza de Cibeles, at the Banco de España headquarters.

## Verdict

The display is lovely, the building is beautiful and the service was good even though we arrived just before closing. Since it's free, it's well worth it if you're near Cibeles. I'd go back, depending on the exhibition.
