Our first walk through central Madrid ended at the Mercado de San Miguel. For me it was a second visit: the first was a year ago, also in November.

## A bit of history

The market opened in May 1916 as a produce market and is one of the finest examples of iron architecture in the city. In May 2009 it became Madrid's first gourmet food market, and today it brings together more than 30 stalls next to Plaza Mayor. *Source: [esMADRID](https://www.esmadrid.com/node/70562) (in Spanish).* Its construction was directed by architect Alfonso Dubé y Díez, and it is the only iron market of its era still standing in the capital. *Source: [Spain.info](https://www.spain.info/es/lugares-interes/mercado-san-miguel/) (in Spanish).*

## The good

- **The place.** It's beautiful, especially at night, with the iron structure lit up.
- **The service.** There was no wait and every stall served us very quickly.
- **The food.** Everything was tasty. Our favorites were the first tapas: patatas bravas and tostas, with an ice-cold Estrella Damm.
- **The desserts.** A nice way to finish while hopping from stall to stall.

## Room for improvement

- **The price.** We spent about €34 per person on 3 drinks, about 4 bites each and desserts. It's a very touristy place and I think the prices reflect that.
- **The baos.** They were our least favorite; we preferred the more traditional tapas.

## Verdict

A beautiful market with tasty food and fast service. It's worth it for tapas-hopping across several stalls, knowing it's geared toward tourists and a bit pricey. I'd go back, and I'd also like to find a similar, more local spot with friendlier prices.
